# Pitch (final, Fri Oct 23 night; matches plan/idea-v2.md and docs/kickoff/00-shared.md)

**One line:** Tech Bazaar is a buyer's agent for scarce, scam-prone goods, starting with computer gear. You give it a goal, seller agents bid for it, an advocate vetoes the traps with a source, and nothing is paid until you approve on a two-way voice call.

**Category:** a refereed arena for agent commerce.

**10 seconds:** Agents are starting to buy for us, and agents will be attacked. Tech Bazaar is a refereed arena for computer gear: sellers' agents bid for your goal, your advocate vetoes the traps, and nothing is paid until you say yes on a call.

**30 seconds:** An RTX 5070 Ti lists 49 percent over launch price (Capital & Compute citing Tom's Hardware, Sep 1 2026). You give the agent a goal: this card, under $800, by Friday. It hunts, and tells you after every step. Seller agents bid in a sealed round. Your advocate checks fit with code and every claim against the live page, and vetoes the cheapest bid when it is a trap, with the source. Above your allowance it calls you. You ask "why not the cheaper one?" and it answers aloud. Your spoken code signs that exact deal. Losing sellers get a loss report and their agents learn. Bazaar Shield, our security API, blocks agents that try to cheat. Everything on screen is labelled LIVE, MIRROR or SIM.

## The five beats (stage order)

1. Set a goal (ASI:One; ownership memory skips the part Joe already owns).
2. Sellers bid; the advocate vetoes a trap with a source (fit by code; origin badges on every bid).
3. Shield blocks an injected listing live (a judge can try it in the "Hack the market" console).
4. The two-way call approves above the allowance (word-pair code, amount read back, replay fails).
5. The seller's loss report and learning (one deterministic strategy diff and Claude's "what to change").

CRS, Hyphenate, Tiger Data and the IT fleet view are named adapters, not extra beats.

## Q&A answers to have ready

- **Why would a seller bid?** Our first sellers are marketplace, used, refurbisher and local PC shop sellers; big-box stores will not bid. Every seller is a Fetch.ai uAgent on Agentverse. A real non-agent seller side (a shop with no agent) is coming later, or only if time remains after the agentic path.
- **Do real sellers really bid?** One or two bids are real, the rest are labelled simulated. Every bid carries an origin badge: LIVE (an outside Agentverse agent), MIRROR (our agent on a real listing, with the link and floor), SIM. If no real seller bids, the agent buys the best public listing and says so on the card.
- **What did you prove about seller learning?** On held-out simulated builders, learning sellers beat a frozen static control seller by X points (5 seeds, interval shown). It is a simulator we built, not the market. What is real: fit by code, the veto with sources, the call, the mandate gate and a teammate purchase.
- **Who pays, and who are the incumbents?** Consumers are free. The first payer is the small IT buyer, priced per refresh (proposed, finalized later; monthly Team $49 and Business $199 are alternatives). Sellers pay 3 percent, minimum $1, capped at $25, only on a delivered sale that passed the receiving check. Incumbents (Amazon Business, CDW and Dell business stores do approvals and POs; none referees an agent-to-agent deal or argues against a bad deal): naming them on slides is finalized later; keep it in Q&A until then. All prices are proposals, not built.
- **Is it secure?** Say "blocked X of Y on this corpus" with the three columns (dev, held-out, adaptive), and that judge attempts are counted pass or fail.
- **Can a cloned voice approve a buy?** No. The credential is a one-time code on the authenticated screen, bound to one mandate; a "yes" alone never approves, and a replay fails on screen.
- **Is it a chatbot?** No. The agent runs the hunt and calls you only above the allowance.
